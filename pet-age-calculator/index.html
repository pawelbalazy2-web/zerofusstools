Why it's not just "times 7"

The old rule of "1 dog year = 7 human years" is a big oversimplification. In reality, pets mature much faster early on — a one-year-old dog is already fully grown, roughly equivalent to a human in their late teens or early twenties — and aging slows down in later years compared to a flat multiplication.

How the result is calculated

This calculator uses an approximate aging curve based on veterinary research: the first year of life equals about 15 human years, the second year adds roughly 9 more, and each following year adds about 4-5 human years, with a slight difference between dogs and cats.

Does breed affect the result?

Yes, in reality — smaller dog breeds tend to live longer and age slower than large breeds. This calculator shows an average value regardless of breed.

Is this a health indicator?

No — it's a fun approximation, not a substitute for veterinary assessment, which accounts for breed, weight, and individual health.
