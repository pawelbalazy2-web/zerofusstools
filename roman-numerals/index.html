How Roman numerals work

The Roman system uses seven symbols: I (1), V (5), X (10), L (50), C (100), D (500), M (1000). Numbers are built by addition (VI = 6) or subtraction when a smaller symbol precedes a larger one (IV = 4, meaning 5 minus 1). This converter supports 1 to 3999 — the standard range for classical Roman notation.

Why is 4 written as IV, not IIII?

Subtractive notation (smaller symbol before larger) is the standard for numbers like 4 and 9, though additive forms like IIII appear historically. This converter uses the standard, widely accepted notation.

What's the maximum number supported?

Classical Roman notation without extended marks supports 1 through 3999.
